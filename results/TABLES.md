## Assumptions and limits

Scores are conditional on finite, seeded, hypothetical applicant populations. The fixed prior gives equal weight to its saved scenarios; it is a modeling choice rather than an empirically calibrated applicant forecast. Game 2 draws all three opponents from the same scenario. The prior Monte Carlo SE combines independent scenario errors as sqrt(sum(SE²))/number of scenarios. Intervals quantify simulation noise only; they exclude uncertainty about applicant behavior, population construction, and candidate selection. Game 1 exact intervals are degenerate because no Monte Carlo is used to compute the expectation. Game 2 rank probabilities divide tied occupied ranks equally and do not give the probability of sole first. Finalists were chosen using search runs; only fresh validation estimates should be quoted as final scores. Search did not establish a global Game 2 optimum, and candidate-set regret may understate true regret. One-million-group checks do not resolve uncertainty about the real applicant pool.
